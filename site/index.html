Who it’s for

For people who use their phone by ear.

Many blind people already use their phones every day, with a screen reader and voice notes. Where they get stuck is the app that wasn’t built for them: an unlabeled button, a gesture menu, a payment screen. Saathi steps in for exactly those moments, then gives control back.

  • Nothing new to learn. Hold a key and speak, the way you’d ask a friend.
  • Works with the phone you already have. Android, the apps you already use, and your own screen reader.
  • You stay in charge. It reads back before anything important and never touches your PIN.

The problem

Screen readers can’t fix apps that weren’t built for them.

Android’s screen reader, TalkBack, only works when an app labels its buttons. Many payment and shopping apps don’t. They use unlabeled icons, gesture menus and CAPTCHAs with no audio option. So millions of blind people still ask a relative to send money for them.

Safety gate
The AI suggests. Plain code decides.

        
Before any tap, a safety check looks at what the AI wants to do. The check is ordinary, tested code, not an AI guess. It lets the action through, holds it until you say yes, or stops it.

Never touches your PIN

          
On a PIN, password or OTP screen, Saathi stops, turns your screen reader back on, and lets you finish.

        

        

          

          
Reads back only what’s on screen

          
The name and amount it reads to you must appear word for word on the screen. Otherwise it asks you.

        

        

          

          
Unclear means cancel

          
If it doesn’t hear a clear yes, it asks once more. Still unclear? It cancels. You can stop it at any moment.

What you can ask
Four everyday jobs, done well.

        
We chose the tasks blind users most often hand to someone else, and made those reliable before adding more apps.

      

      

        

          Core
          WhatsApp
          
“Read Mummy’s last message.”

          
Or in Hinglish: “Mummy ka last message padho”

          
Reply by voice. It reads back who it’s going to and the exact words before sending.

        

        

          CoreComing later

“Send Rahul ₹1.”

          
Or in Hindi: “राहुल को एक रुपया भेजो”

          
Asks which Rahul if there are two, reads back the payment, then hands over for your PIN.

“Order 1 litre of Amul milk.”

          
Or in Hinglish: “Amul doodh 1 litre mangao”

New, in beta: Walk mode. Point the phone’s camera ahead and hear Hindi warnings with a rough distance, like “सामने दरवाज़ा, लगभग 3 मीटर” (door ahead, about 3 metres). It runs entirely on the phone, works offline, and only ever warns: it never says the way is safe. An aid alongside a cane, not a replacement.
